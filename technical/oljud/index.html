oljud

sound blaster 16 driver

oljud is moonshot's sound driver. it drives a sound blaster 16 and plays whatever a ring-3 program hands it through sound(buf, len), one system call. samples come at 22050 a second. a program starts with 8-bit mono, where 128 is silence, so it can build sound with nothing but store8. sound_format(1) switches it to 16-bit mono and sound_format(2) to 16-bit stereo, left then right. the card itself plays 16-bit stereo. mono is heard the same on both sides.

why this card

the sound blaster 16 is an isa card from 1992. its age is the point. everything about it sits at a fixed address, port 0x220, interrupt 5 and the old isa dma controller. newer audio chips sit on the pci bus and have to be found by walking it first. qemu emulates the card, while people still own the real thing.

how it plays

the dma controller loops over a buffer of two halves on its own. the card interrupts at the end of each half. while it reads one half, the interrupt handler refills the other with what programs have queued, or with silence.

every program has a queue of its own, about three seconds long. sound copies as much as fits and returns how much that was. sound(0, 0) returns how much is still waiting and sound(0, -1) stops the program's sound at once. the queues of all programs are mixed, so two programs are heard together.

the sounds moonshot makes itself

sign-in plays a short sound, made by the program fanfar.elf with no window of its own. a window opens and closes with a soft cue. the word tyst on the kernel line in grub turns the cues off.
